The case

A tech company is offering an AI-powered learning assistant to our college. It tracks each student’s progress, suggests personalised content and alerts faculty when a student may fall behind. The college is considering using it in every department.

The promise is better learning efficiency and academic performance. The worry, from both students and faculty, is data privacy, over-reliance on AI and bias in recommendations. The question is how to adopt the tool while answering those worries.

The plan: pilot first, protect always

The college does not launch everywhere at once. It runs one careful semester and lets the results decide.

  1. Before launch

    Pick two departments, one technical and one non-technical. Agree the success measures in writing. Sign privacy terms with the company. Tell students exactly what is collected.

  2. Weeks one to six

    The pilot runs with the minimum data: marks and quiz results only. Every alert goes to a faculty member before anything else happens.

  3. Mid-semester check

    Compare alert rates across school background and gender. A student and faculty feedback committee meets and shares problems.

  4. End of semester

    Data is deleted unless students agree to keep it. Results are compared with the targets. The college then decides.

Eight safeguards that stay on throughout

Collect the minimum
Only data that is truly needed. Nothing about habits or typing patterns.
Students see first
Each student reads their own report before faculty do, and can ask for corrections.
Consent without penalty
Taking part is a choice. Saying no costs nothing.
A person decides
An alert only starts a conversation. No label goes on a record.
Audit for bias
If one group is flagged far more often, the pilot pauses until the company explains why.
Privacy in the contract
The company may not train its other products on college data, and must delete it when the contract ends.
Protected tasks
Projects and open discussions stay free of the tool, so independent thinking keeps its place.
Delete by default
Old data does not sit around for years waiting to be misused.

The decision

If the targets are met

Expand slowly, one department at a time, and keep every safeguard. Success means flagged students really needed help, students rate the experience well and the bias audit shows no large gaps.

If they are not met

Use the alternative plan. Any one of these can be chosen:

  • Keep personalised content and switch off risk alerts.
  • Let students use the tool privately, with nothing shared with faculty.
  • Choose another vendor, or build a smaller tool inside the college.
  • Spend the money on more mentors and tutors instead.

Four views the plan has to respect

Students
Want help without being watched.
Faculty
Want support without losing their judgement.
Administration
Wants better results at a fair cost.
The company
Wants wide adoption.
